# Controlled follow-ups

Open **Outreach Logs → Follow-ups awaiting review** after pulling this update and restarting both servers. No new credentials, requirements, models or paid services are needed.

1. Send eligible initial outreach through the existing Leads and Approvals workflow. Connection-test messages are excluded. Only Gmail-confirmed initial outreach linked to a currently qualified lead can be considered.
2. Wait seven elapsed days (168 hours) from the recorded initial submission attempt. The dashboard displays the availability time in SAST. This is a fixed pilot limit, with **one follow-up maximum per mailbox/conversation**. Waiting alone does not trigger an action unless you explicitly enable the draft schedule described below.
3. Use **Settings → Gmail → Sync replies**, then return to Outreach Logs and click **Refresh eligibility**. The successful sync must be no more than 15 minutes old, no later than the current time, and after the initial outreach. Sync before preparing and again before approving if necessary. The app only knows about replies it has downloaded.
4. Select an eligible conversation and click **Prepare follow-up draft**. Read and edit the message. It uses a local scope-discussion template and the saved agency portfolio, with no inferred prices or results. The original Gmail thread and Message-ID determine the recipient/thread/subject.
5. Click **Save for approval**, open **Approvals**, and review the exact saved text before approving. Eligibility is checked when preparing, queueing, approving and immediately before submission. Later portfolio edits do not rewrite queued content. A changed original message, lead review or catalogue invalidates an unqueued preview.

Any recorded incoming message in the conversation blocks this no-response workflow, including automated notices, opt-outs and bounces. Review these conversations through reply handling instead. An explicit interested/declined/won/unsubscribed outcome, suppressed contact, stale/invalid lead review, uncertain/in-progress submission or another active thread draft also blocks it. An initial message without a linked reviewed lead, provider confirmation or original thread/Message-ID is unavailable. Do not import contact guesses to bypass these checks.

Repeated/concurrent queue actions retain one approval and never overwrite its text. Once a follow-up approval exists, use its existing edit, reject or reconciliation controls; generating again does not create a second follow-up. Rejected follow-ups are not recreated. Failed submissions retain the existing explicit retry rules, and uncertain sends require reconciliation. The individual controls do not schedule work. The optional draft schedule below can queue the same local template, while sending still requires explicit approval.

The submitted message keeps `followup_of` provenance. Reports count a successful follow-up as another sent email within the same conversation, so the conversation denominator is not doubled. Existing saved snapshots remain unchanged. Gmail acceptance still does not prove inbox placement, reading or opening.

Your owned-mailbox connection test and acknowledgment replies validate connection/reply handling, but will not appear here. Live follow-up eligibility requires actual qualified outreach with recorded permission and the waiting period. Offline tests cover the timing boundary, fresh sync, blocking evidence, stale previews, once-only queueing/dispatch, source scoping, correct threading and report counts using fake providers. No real business emails were sent for implementation testing.

Private endpoints: `GET /api/followups`, `POST /api/followups/{outbound_id}/preview`, `POST /api/followups/{outbound_id}/draft` with a preview ID and edited body. Existing API-key protection applies; candidates and actions are scoped to the configured mailbox. Legacy approvals retain their earlier checks. Keep the local SQLite database available and backed up; this remains a single-host pilot.

## Optional daily draft schedule

Open **Outreach Logs → Scheduled follow-up drafts**. It starts **paused** for each mailbox. Enabling it covers all current and future eligible conversations in that mailbox; it does not grant contact permission. Start with batch size **1**, choose a SAST time and whether to use weekdays only, then save. The backend must remain running. `FOLLOWUP_SCHEDULES_ENABLED=true` starts the local worker by default, but it does nothing until a schedule is explicitly enabled. Set it to `false` and restart to disable the worker; explicit manual batch preparation remains available.

The worker checks every 30 seconds. At or after the scheduled time, it prepares at most one batch of 1–3 follow-up drafts per mailbox per SAST day, oldest eligible conversation first. This cap is separate from the installation's ten campaign drafting attempts and business-send limits. Individual manual follow-up controls remain available and are not counted as scheduled batches. No Gmail, Tavily or model request is made by the follow-up scheduler, and it never sends email.

A successful Gmail sync must be recorded within the last 15 minutes, and must include each original thread. This worker does not perform that sync. Without fresh sync or eligible conversations it waits without claiming a batch. If Gmail polling is already configured, its normal sync can satisfy this requirement; otherwise sync in Settings. The app only knows downloaded replies. Every original follow-up guard still applies when preparing, queueing and later approving/sending, including current qualification and permission, seven elapsed days, one follow-up per conversation, reply/notice/bounce/outcome blocks and held submissions.

The card shows currently eligible recipients, saved next-due time and recent batch history. **Prepare today’s batch now** uses the enabled schedule and its saved batch size, overriding time/weekday only. It shares the once-per-day claim with the automatic worker and still requires fresh sync and eligible conversations. A manual click may return no batch if eligibility changed. Review the exact generated messages in Approvals before sending.

Changing or pausing the schedule stops subsequent members of an active batch. A member already being prepared may finish; pause does not reject or delete existing approvals. Business sending pause is separate and does not prevent draft preparation. Saving an enabled schedule chooses the next future scheduled time; missed dates are not replayed. If today's batch was already claimed, editing the time, pausing/resuming, changing batch size or restarting cannot create another batch today. Skipped or unfinished members do not get an extra same-day batch.

Batch claims and run history are durable. Concurrent workers/manual clicks share a lease and one daily claim. A stop leaves a partial history; a failure or recovered interrupted run pauses the schedule. Review its recorded results and existing Approvals before re-enabling. A crash after saving an approval may leave that approval without a result row; the original once-only follow-up key prevents recreating it. Failed or interrupted batches are not replayed automatically. The next allowed batch considers remaining eligible conversations after review.

Additional private routes: `GET /api/followups/schedule`, `PATCH /api/followups/schedule` (version, paused, local_time, weekdays_only, batch_size), and `POST /api/followups/schedule/run`. Existing API-key protection and current-mailbox scoping apply. Pull and restart both servers; no new dependencies or credentials are needed. Offline regressions validate the implementation; a real seven-day business follow-up still needs operator validation.
